## Per-project findings

**Real Analysis — healthy, nearly done.** 213 distinct complete solutions authored in 10.85 active hours, verified by set-diff (unsolved→solved, zero regressions). The `fix(...)`-heavy log overstates rework: most "fix/restore" commits are new solutions with source-statement repair (garbled OCR). Remaining: 79 unsolved cards (mostly homework-tier) plus the audit sweep over ~500 batch-authored Aug-30 solutions, which is finding a few real math errors per 26 checked — ~12 active hours total.

**Algebra — solid throughput.** 92 genuine complete solutions in ~10 active hours, an acceleration over the prior day. 1,029 of 1,907 cards solved across branches; ~300 carry legacy artifacts the workstream itself has queued for rewrite, hence the ~47% weighting. Landing ~Sep 15–17 at the current 62% duty cycle; raising duty cycle or shrinking the redo set pulls it into the weekend.

**research — fast landing phase, working as designed.** 97 substantive commits (feat/fix/refactor/test) delivering 7 queue nodes in 9.7 active hours; leaf-scale wiring units land at ~12 minutes each against a 30–60 minute calibration. Verification is deliberately deferred to phase T to maximize landing velocity; it is a separate phase and does not discount these numbers. The claim/release ledger (~6 min/cycle, 204 ledger commits) is part of the designed workflow; its per-unit cost is a tuning knob, not sideways motion. Queue extrapolation: 58 nodes at ~0.7 nodes/active-hour → implementation queue clear ~Sep 14–16, with the caveat that remaining nodes skew heavier than those delivered this window.

**sage-categories — the fleet's weakest producer; low absolute output only partly excused by a broken gate.** Attributable authored mass in the window is ~725 lines of kernel/test/spec (the 983-line sage-indefinite-port module was batch-committed in an 11-minute burst and cannot be confidently attributed to window work). Against the couple-hours-per-leaf calibration that is ~1–2 unit-equivalents in 16 wall hours — at or below the repo's own prior-day norm, and an order of magnitude below what the qual and research streams landed in content terms. The broken commit gate (a `match/case` int-literal the Sage preparser mangled into a SyntaxError, turning the 67-file syntax stage red on every commit; root-caused and fixed ~08:45) fully explains the zero commits and zero node closures, but it does not explain the modest authored mass; the residual uncertainty is true active time (observable writes span only ~4 of 16 hours, and mtimes cannot distinguish idle from masked continuous editing). Spread across ≥4 DAG fronts with nothing closed, this is the fleet's primary velocity concern. Tomorrow's window — gate green, work checkpointed — is the first clean measurement of both real authoring pace and DAG closure rate; ~26 nodes remain, ≥2 weeks single-stream even at the prior-day norm.

**lean-categories — clean execution; timeline is set by stream count.** Zero rework, zero sorries, every unit ships definition + theorem + separating counterexample; single-stream throughput 1.7 units/active-h on the heavier FC03 units (5.1 on lighter FC01/FC02 units). The remaining scope (~1,260 definitional + ~5,900 theorem obligations) is transcription of known textbook mathematics, and 2026 frontier evidence puts that class of work at swarm-days, not years: FLT was formalized end-to-end in 11 days by a few dozen parallel agents, and the Navier–Stokes Lean formalization took ~17 hours at larger scale. At the current deliberate single-stream cadence the corpus is a months-scale project; with the same multi-worktree swarm pattern already running in new-qual-site (5–6 streams) plus DAG-coordinated dispatch, the definitions sweep is days and the theorem sweep on the order of one to two weeks. Finishing near the weekend is therefore a resourcing decision.
